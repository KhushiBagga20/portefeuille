Recently, I had a weird dream.

In said dream, I was in a railway station, one of those confusing, liminal ones that feel bigger on the inside than they should be. I was there with my family, planning to travel somewhere, and at some point I was asked to retrieve something one of them had misplaced. So I ran toward a staircase that looked less like stairs and more like a maze, spiraling far higher than I expected it to.\
On my way back, I saw a “friend”,someone I wouldn’t expect to see in any dream at all. I said hi to their family, left, and somehow found myself sent back again to retrieve something else. The staircase was the same, but *not quite*. Then I woke up.

Immediately upon waking up I was rather confused. What a random and weird dream? It didn't make sense, it wasn't connected but it existed with such confidence that I believed it was real at that moment. Then it hit me.

I was dreaming.

Dreams are hallucinations that occur during sleep, these happen involuntarily. While the exact "why" is still a subject of scientific debate, we have a strong understanding of "what" is happening physically and "when" it occurs. REM sleep occurs in cycles of 90 minutes and is characterized by vivid dreams, high brain activity, and muscle paralysis. NREM dreams are usually dull and repetitive, similar to mere thinking.

A particularly fascinating study from the University of California, Berkeley suggests that dreaming plays a crucial role in emotional processing. REM sleep suppresses stress hormones, enabling the brain to revisit difficult or traumatic experiences calmly. This process gradually removes the emotional charge from memories, making them easier to recall later without overwhelming feeling.

But you didn't click on this article to read about sleep science. You came here for the AI. This is answered by the “Activation-synthesis” hypothesis. This theory argues that dreams don't start with a meaning, instead the brain stem fires random electrical signals during sleep, our cortex (the thinking part) tries to make sense of this chaos by stitching it into a coherent story(synthesis). Our brain just tries to rationalize random neural noise.

Much like AI trying to find a pattern in static.

Before explaining why AI systems hallucinate, it helps to understand why humans do—or rather, why we dream.\
So how do AI large language models actually work?\
In the most disrespectful but technically accurate terms: they’re extremely sophisticated autocorrect algorithms. Language models generate text by predicting the next word based on probability, drawing from patterns learned across a vast dataset. After seeing how words tend to appear together in different contexts, the model learns statistical relationships between them.

Upon reading almost every book on the internet, you will eventually find out that certain words are used in a certain context. For every query, the LLMS play an intricate game of fill-in-the blanks with probability. The only thing that's different in an LLM and autocorrect is attention, it looks at the entire sentence before predicting the next word. Crucially, though, it doesn’t fact-check. It optimizes for plausibility, not truth. When that plausibility drifts away from reality, the result is factually wrong information, which is then referred to as a hallucination.

But hey, I've always wondered why when an AI model gives factually incorrect information it's called a hallucination and not an error? But AI models spew nonsense with confidence and structure which is rather strange. Now, the model is obviously working as intended but the output is falsy, this creates a discrepancy in what the wrong action actually is. It's not a bug in programming, the code works perfectly, did what it was meant to do and predicted whatever the next word was according to its training. A traditional software error is a failure of function. An AI hallucination is a failure of fact, usually delivered with perfect function.

This results in it shifting from actual reality which mirrors the biological definition of hallucination, a sensory experience created by the mind not by external stimuli. AI hallucinations are persuasive and confident, it fills gaps when it doesn’t know, inventing details instead of admitting uncertainty.

AI models constantly make up stuff and can be wrong multiple times but since that's the way they are engineered to be, their errors are supposedly considered a feature instead of a flaw. Increase their randomness—commonly referred to as temperature—and you get more unexpected, creative, and sometimes nonsensical outputs. Lower it, and responses become safer but duller. Creativity and hallucination sit uncomfortably close together. You cannot have the poet without the liar.

A large-scale study conducted by researchers at Université de Montréal explored this relationship by comparing creativity in humans and AI models using the Divergent Association Task (DAT). Participants were asked to generate 10 words that were as semantically distant from one another as possible. Now, the results are fascinating, AI models consistently scored higher than average humans.

However, the top human performers with highest creative percentiles significantly outperformed the best AI models. Which led to the revelation that AI can simulate creativity impressively, but it hasn’t surpassed the deepest, most original forms of human creativity.

And this leads us to the prediction paradox.

We want our AI systems to be creative, truthful, and human-like. But to achieve this, we built them to predict, to bridge gaps, and to infer meaning—just like the human brain does while dreaming. In doing so, we’ve inevitably taught them how to lie.\
Think of it as digital pareidolia.\
Pareidolia is the psychological phenomenon that causes humans to see faces in electrical sockets or animals in clouds. Our brains are hardwired to find recognizable shapes in chaos, in everything we can. AI models suffer from a hyper-active, mathematical version of this. When an LLM looks at a prompt that has no clear answer, it doesn't see a void; it sees a shape. It sees the structure of an answer.\
Because it is rewarded for completion and penalized for silence, the model forces a connection where none exists. It doesn’t just see a face in the cloud; it confidently writes a biography for that face and dates that never happened. It builds the staircase in any way it can, because statistically, a staircase should be there, even if it leads nowhere.\
But if human dreams are the recycling of our daily emotions—a way to strip the "charge" from our memories—what exactly is AI processing?

It is dreaming the collective unconscious of the internet.

The "memories" of an AI are not its own experiences, but a flattened, weighted average of humanity's digital exhaust. It has ingested every Reddit argument, every piece of fanfiction, every contradictory scientific abstract, and every confident lie posted on a forum since 2005. When it hallucinates, it is often just mirroring the confident ignorance it found in a comment section three terabytes ago. It synthesizes our collective noise into a single, persuasive voice.\
This creates a dilemma for engineers who are now trying to induce a state of "Lucid Dreaming" in these machines.\
In sleep science, a lucid dreamer is someone who realizes they are dreaming and can take control of the narrative. In AI, we call this Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF). We are effectively trying to teach the model to "wake up" mid-sentence, to recognize when it is drifting from fact into fiction, and to stop itself. We want it to say, "I don't know," rather than inventing a convenient truth.

This is what makes the Prediction Paradox dangerous. A dream that feels real is harder to wake up from. A falsehood that is plausible, fluent, and confident is far more persuasive than one that is obviously broken.\
If we penalize a model for hallucinations, we do more than correct error—we restrict the space in which it is allowed to think. Accuracy becomes a boundary. The demand for perfect correctness begins to resemble an instruction to a poet to abandon metaphor and speak only in what can be verified.\
Such a system may be safer and more reliable, but it will also be less capable of surprise. As precision tightens, imagination recedes. What remains is an intelligence that is exact and obedient, yet curiously hollow—awake, articulate, and incapable of dreaming.\
I’ve never been to a railway station like the one in my dream. I’ve never seen a staircase like that. I don’t even like that friend or their family. And my family would never send me back into a maze to retrieve something they never misplaced.

It was all nonsense.

But in that moment, that is the reality my brain believes in. After all, the wildest, most nonsensical and most brilliant dreams remain uniquely human.
